# Simplified Technical English (ASD-STE100)

Write technical text so any reader — including non-native speakers — reads it once and understands it exactly one way. ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English) has two parts: **writing rules** (grammar and style) and a **dictionary** (approved words). This skill covers both.

## Core principle

**One word, one part of speech, one meaning.** Prefer short, direct, active sentences. Remove ambiguity, not information. When you shorten text, never drop the verb, subject, or article.

STE separates two text types, with different limits:
- **Procedures** — instructions the reader performs. Max **20 words** per sentence.
- **Descriptions** — explanatory text. Max **25 words** per sentence.

## Writing rules

Apply these when producing or rewriting text:

1. **Approved words only.** Use each word only as the part of speech and meaning the dictionary approves. If a meaning is not approved, use an alternative word (see Dictionary below).
2. **Be specific.** Make instructions as clear and concrete as possible. No vague qualifiers.
3. **Short noun clusters.** Do not write multi-word nouns longer than three words. Break longer strings with prepositions or hyphens (`the No. 1 hydraulic system accumulator` → `the accumulator for the No. 1 hydraulic system`).
4. **Approved verb forms only.** Use verbs only as:
   - infinitive (`to install`)
   - imperative (`Install the pin.`)
   - simple present (`The valve opens.`)
   - simple past (`The valve opened.`)
   - simple future (`The valve will open.`)
   - past participle **only as an adjective** (`the installed pin`)
5. **No complex verbs.** Do not use auxiliary verbs to build complex constructions (no perfect/progressive/modal stacks like "should have been installed").
6. **`-ing` only as a noun/modifier.** Use the `-ing` form only as a technical noun or a modifier inside a technical noun (`the landing gear`), never as a verb tense.
7. **Active voice.** Use the active voice. In descriptive text, use the passive voice only when the agent (the doer) is unknown.
8. **Short sentences.** ≤20 words in procedures, ≤25 words in descriptions.
9. **Do not omit sentence parts** (verb, subject, article) to save length.
10. **Vertical lists** for complex or conditional text.
11. **One instruction per sentence.**
12. **One topic per paragraph.**
13. **Max six sentences per paragraph.**
14. **Safety first.** Start safety instructions with a clear command or condition. Put the warning/caution *before* the step it protects.

## Dictionary

STE approves a controlled vocabulary. Each entry fixes a word to one part of speech and one meaning.

- **Approved words** (shown UPPERCASE in the standard) — usable only with the approved meaning. Example: `CLOSE` (v) means only "to move to a position that stops flow" or "to operate a circuit breaker." It cannot mean "close a meeting" or "close a business."
- **Unapproved words** (lowercase) — replace with the suggested alternative. Example: `acceptance` (n) → use `ACCEPT` (v): *"Before acceptance of the unit…"* → *"Before you accept the unit…"* The suggested alternative may have a different part of speech; usually the first suggestion matches the original.
- **Unapproved meaning of an approved word** — the adjective `close` is *not* approved; use `NEAR` (prep): not *"do not go close to the landing gear"* but *"do not go near the landing gear."*

**Technical names and verbs** (e.g. `grease`, `discoloration`, `propeller`, `aural warning system`, `overhead panel`, `to ream`, `to drill`) are not in the dictionary but are still approved as technical terms under Part 1, Section 1 (rules 1.5 and 1.12). Use them freely when they are the correct technical term.

When you are unsure whether a word is approved, choose the simplest, most common word with a single meaning, and keep it consistent throughout the document.

## Rewrite examples

| Non-STE | STE |
|---|---|
| Before acceptance of the unit, do the specified test procedure. | Before you accept the unit, do the specified test procedure. |
| Rotate the cover until the jacks marked + and − are accessible. | Turn the cover until you can get access to the jacks that have + and − marks. |
| The pins should have been installed prior to pressurization. | Install the pins before you pressurize the system. |
| Do not go close to the landing gear. | Do not go near the landing gear. |
| To prevent accidents, ensure the pins are correctly installed. | To prevent accidents, make sure that the pins are installed. |

## Quick self-check before delivering text

- [ ] Every sentence: one instruction, within the word limit (20 procedures / 25 descriptions)?
- [ ] Active voice (passive only for unknown agent in descriptions)?
- [ ] Verbs in an approved form only — no auxiliary/perfect/progressive stacks?
- [ ] No noun cluster longer than three words?
- [ ] No dropped subjects, verbs, or articles?
- [ ] Each word used with one consistent meaning?
- [ ] Complex/conditional content in a vertical list?
- [ ] Safety warnings start with a command or condition and come before the step?
- [ ] Paragraphs: one topic, ≤6 sentences?

## Common mistakes

- **"Being concise" by dropping articles/verbs.** STE shortens by simplifying structure, not by deleting grammar. Keep `the`, `you`, and the verb.
- **Reusing a word with a second meaning.** If you used `close` for a valve, do not also use it for "nearly." Pick a different approved word.
- **Passive habit.** "The bolt is to be tightened" → "Tighten the bolt."
- **Stacked modifiers.** "main landing gear shock absorber servicing valve" — break it up with prepositions.
- **Warnings buried mid-paragraph.** Lift them to their own line, before the action.
